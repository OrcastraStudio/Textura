# Shared style and fiction capabilities

This source edition retains the 0.1.2 module design while excluding personal decision evidence, author reports, private record identifiers and workflow presets. The following describes supported protocol choices, not universal tastes or consent on behalf of a new author.

## Style

- Authors explicitly select sample versions and purposes. Selection for analysis does not make every observed feature a preferred norm.
- Keep observations, author-confirmed preferences, provisional goals and proposed experiments separate, with examples, counterexamples and scope.
- Optional author, work, narrator and character layers support distinct voices across language, genre, passage and situation.
- Reference authors support close reading, comparison and adaptation. Admiration is not an instruction to imitate or infer psychology from a text.
- Study sentence-level expression and whole-work composition. Preserve translation/edition attribution; distinguish translator choices from inspected original wording.
- Actual-work analysis, independent practice and combinations are available without mandatory priority. Writing owns practice prose.
- Optional statistics need actual inputs, a stated method and comparability limits. A number is not a literary quality score. The current module supplies a protocol and template, not an automatic statistics engine.

## Fiction

- Support short fiction through long novels. Begin with fragments, events, characters, relationships, settings or a manuscript; a complete outline is not required.
- Compare possible centers, selection/order and single-center or multi-center structures. Preserve alternatives with gains, tradeoffs, missing links and uncertainty, without a universal scoring system.
- Label new material and transformations as proposals. Adoption into a plan and incorporation into a manuscript are distinct events.
- Permit progressive planning or other author-selected methods. Do not invent a fixed ending, prescribed plot form or default aesthetic objective.
- Keep branches distinct and distinguish an unresolved point from deliberate ambiguity.
- Separate fictional events/settings, narrative assertions, character knowledge/belief and information disclosed to readers. One layer does not automatically establish another.
- After important adopted changes, map plausible impacts, check by priority and retain unreviewed scope. Evidence of contradiction differs from hypotheses about artistic effect.
- Preserve conflicting manuscript and setting statements with exact versions. Do not silently choose newer text, older settings or library records.
- First-reader responses and full-context editorial review have distinct input boundaries. Simulated perspectives do not establish real-audience statistics.
- Shared settings are optional. Each work adopts an explicit obtainable version; availability of a newer version never changes adoption automatically.

## Ownership and execution

Style owns observations and sample references; Fiction owns structure and work-local story records; Writing owns prose; Review owns frozen context and responses; State owns actual author decisions and handoffs. LibraryBridge owns acquired external setting references and prepared transfer packets.

The executable Review workflow freezes Writing/Evidence inputs only. Extended fiction context, statistics and impact checks are agent-managed protocols. A future formal shared-setting provider needs an actual adapter; no such write service is supplied.

Blank templates are not completed records. No creative corpus, mature style profile, personal aesthetic preference or actual fiction project is bundled.
