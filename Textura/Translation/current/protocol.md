# Cross-language operating protocol

1. Identify the source version, original language and precise passage/locator. Check whether the available text is itself a translation or an imperfect extraction.
2. Preserve original passages needed to support the reading. Attribute a published translation, human translation or AI-assisted rendering accurately; never present a new AI rendering as a published translator's words.
3. Provide an explanation in the author's useful reading language. Pair important terms and quotations with the original and explain material alternatives.
4. Preserve ambiguity where appropriate. Mark uncertainty caused by language competence, historical terminology, missing context, damaged text or OCR. Do not invent a confident reconstruction.
5. Check whether the proposed manuscript claim still matches the source's scope, modality, tense, qualifiers and context after translation. Send claim-level judgments to Evidence, with the translation record linked.
6. For a quotation, retain the exact cited version and identify whether the quoted text is original or translated. Do not create source page numbers for an extracted or reformatted derivative.
7. Carry local-only restrictions forward. A restricted original cannot be sent to a cloud translation tool simply because it is in another language.
8. Treat works written in different languages as independent unless the author establishes a translation/adaptation relation. Source comprehension notes do not create such a work relation automatically.

When reliable interpretation is unavailable, record a bounded tentative reading and the need for qualified checking; do not certify the claim.

## Default presentation in a public work

For a quotation whose language differs from the work language, place the translation in the body and the corresponding original, source and translator or translation method in notes. Include key original terms inline when they materially help interpretation. Keep the original/translation association and exact version/locator internally regardless of display. Published translations must be attributed accurately; new human or AI-assisted renderings must not inherit a published translator credit. Apply explicit author exceptions per work and pass the presentation details to Publishing.
